`ideas/flame-bulb.md`.

## Not needed (for the record)

- No buck converter: the XIAO runs from the 5 V rail directly (5 V pin).
- No battery, no charger, no MOSFET rail switch — the tea light's power-gating
  is irrelevant on mains.
- No BTF connectors: solder directly, the strips are cut segments anyway.

## Power budget

100 px on a 2 A supply. Cap brightness in firmware at **25–30 %**: a flame
effect then averages ~1.5 A; all-channels-full white at 100 % would draw ~5.8 A
and is what the cap exists to prevent. Design point ≤1.5 A continuous so the
Hi-Link stays cool inside a closed body.

## Order of assembly

See `flame-bulb-exploded.png` (generated by `flame-bulb-exploded.py`).

1. Mains: E26 plug leads → fuse → Hi-Link AC-in. Heat-shrink everything.
2. Drop the Hi-Link into the base upright; route 5 V out through the wire hole in the top face.
3. Epoxy the E26 plug into the cap's cup (leads first through the floor hole); press the cap into the base bottom.
4. Wire the XIAO (5 V, GND), level shifter and cap in the core bore; data out via the wire slot.
5. Stick a 10-px strip on each of the 10 facets, all data-in ends at the bottom; daisy-chain data column to column; star-feed 5 V/GND from a ring at the bottom.
6. Plug the core into the base's hex socket; slide the shade down over the core onto the base spigot.
